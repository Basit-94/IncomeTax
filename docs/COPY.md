# Wapsi — copy rules

The interface teaches, or the user calls a CA. Copy is not decoration here; for the first-time filer
it *is* the product.

## Six rules

1. **Never leave an acronym bare.** Expand it in place, the first time, on every screen it appears.
   The user did not read your earlier screen.
2. **Name things the way the user would.** "Tax your employer already paid" beats "TDS". Keep the
   official term alongside it, because they will see it on the government site later — but lead with
   the human phrasing.
3. **Money is the user's, not the system's.** "Coming back to you" beats "Net position −6,400".
   Frame every figure as what it means for their money.
4. **A control says what will happen.** "File this return" beats "Submit". After it happens, say what
   happens *next* — a reference number alone answers nothing.
5. **Tell them what to expect, including time.** "Your refund usually arrives in 2–5 weeks" is the
   question every filer actually has, and almost no product answers it.
6. **Disputing must feel cheap.** "This looks wrong" invites a correction. "I dispute this" sounds
   like the start of a legal process, and people won't click it.

## Register varies by direction — vocabulary does not

The twelve directions deliberately differ in voice: The Chamber is formal, Tape is technical, Receipt
is terse. **That stays.** What is standardised is the vocabulary and the information: no direction
leaves jargon unexplained, and none ends a filing with a bare reference number.

## Glossary — the phrasing to use

| Official term | Lead with | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Assessment year 2026–27 | "the tax year you're filing for" | Gloss it once per page; nobody knows which year AY 2026–27 means |
| ITR-1 | "the form for salaried people" | Keep the code as a secondary label |
| TDS | "tax your employer already paid for you" | The single most misunderstood item on a return |
| Form 16 | "your employer's salary statement" | Recognisable enough to keep the name |
| AIS | "the yearly statement your bank files" | Almost nobody knows this acronym |
| Section 80C | "life insurance and provident fund" | Lead with what it covers, keep the section number |
| Cess | "a 4% health and education surcharge" | Explain every time; it is never self-evident |
| Slab rates | "the rate bands your income falls into" | — |
| Standard deduction | keep the name, add "every salaried person gets this" | It is a name, but the entitlement needs saying |
| Regime (old/new) | "tax scheme" | And always say which is better *for them*, with the reason |
| Refund | "coming back to you" | — |

## Filing confirmation

Never end on a reference number alone. The pattern:

> **Filed.** Your refund usually arrives in 2–5 weeks. Reference WPS-2026-4471.

Three things in order: it worked, what happens next and when, then the reference for their records.

## Errors

State what went wrong, then what to do. Never apologise, never blame the user.

> Bad: "Submission failed."
> Good: "We couldn't file this — your draft is saved. Try again in a moment."
