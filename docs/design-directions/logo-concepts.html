Five marks, all built as vectors

Every mark is pure SVG: it inherits currentColor, so it flips with the theme; it stays crisp at 16 px; and it can be nudged to the pixel. Each is shown at display size, as a lockup, and at true favicon size — because a mark that dies at 16 px is not a logo, it is an illustration.

The idea underneath all five: वापसी means return. That is the whole product in one word — a tax return, and your money coming back. Every mark below is some form of a line that turns around and comes home.

1 · The Return

recommended

A single stroke leaves, turns, and comes back carrying the arrowhead. The gap at the top is the year you wait; the arrowhead is the refund landing. Simplest at 16 px — one closed gesture, no interior detail to collapse. Reads as a "W" on second glance without ever announcing it.

2 · The Split

most honest

The proportion bar from the product itself: what stays yours, what went to tax, what comes back. The small green piece has broken rank and is returning. This mark is a data visualisation — it states the thesis rather than decorating it. Weakest at 16 px, where the thin segment fuses; the favicon uses a two-part reduction.

3 · वा

most Indian

The Devanagari वा — the first syllable of वापसी — with its headline (shirorekha) extended into the returning arrow. Unmistakably of this country, which generic fintech blue never is. The risk is honest: it reads instantly to a Devanagari reader and as an abstract glyph to everyone else.

4 · The Ledger

most institutional

Three ruled lines of an account book. The bottom rule refuses to end — it hooks back on itself. This is the append-only ledger drawn as a monogram: entries stack downward, and the correction returns. Quiet, official, and it survives a rubber stamp or an embossed letterhead.

5 · The Rupee Turn

most literal

The ₹ sign's two horizontals kept, its leg swung into a return arc. Money, coming back, in one glyph — the least explaining required. Also the most conventional, and ₹ marks are crowded in Indian fintech, so it wins recognition and loses distinctiveness.

How I would choose

opinion

If you want the mark that works hardest and breaks least, take 1 · The Return. One stroke, one colour accent, legible at any size, and it never needs explaining.

If you want the mark that says what the product believes, take 2 · The Split. It is the only one that is literally a chart of the user's year — and this product's whole argument is that a number should show its working.

If you want the mark that could only be Indian, take 3 · वा. Pair it with 1 as the favicon and you get both.
