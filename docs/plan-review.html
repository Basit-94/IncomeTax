This document is the build plan for Wapsi (वापसी — the return, and your money coming back): a rebuild of the income tax e-filing journey for the people who use it most and are served by it least. Round one closes 28 Aug 2026, 20:00 IST. There is no grace period.

Read it top to bottom. Everything marked as it could be is a working demonstration, not a description.

Part A General the problem, shown

The most-read sentence on the portal says nothing.

It appears when you file. When you verify. When you open your annual statement. When you download a receipt. In the mobile app. It is the same eight words every time, and it has never once told anybody what to do next.

Part B The journey three acts, one problem

One journey: from “I owe nothing” to “the money is in my account.”

The brief asks for one clearly defined problem. This is one problem with three moments in it. A journey that ends at submit would not be a citizen journey at all — it would stop exactly where the citizen's actual difficulty begins.

As it could be

Check this — you don't have to fill it in

Your pay last year

₹4,20,000

Chettinad Textiles Pvt Ltd reported this to the department on 12 May 2026

TAN CMBC12874E

What should it say?

Only Chettinad Textiles can change this at the source. We'll send them exactly what to correct, and tell you when they've done it. You don't have to argue with the department about someone else's paperwork.

You owe nothing. ₹8,400 comes back to you.

Both buttons work. So does the language switch.

The provenance line is the invention. The real portal shows figures with no origin, so a citizen who spots an error cannot tell whose mistake they are arguing with — and a Form 26AS error can only be corrected by the deductor filing a correction statement. Naming the reporter is what makes “just confirm this” a fair thing to ask of someone who is frightened of getting a government form wrong.

This review document loads a webfont. The shipped product deliberately loads none. A Tamil-reading user on a slow connection should not download 200 KB before she can read her refund status, so the product uses the system stack and spends the budget on scale and spacing instead. IBM Plex is here because it is the open family with the widest Devanagari and Tamil coverage, which lets this page demonstrate the trilingual claim rather than assert it.

Act 2

When something comes back

Rakesh Kumar · Lucknow

His broker reported the total value of everything he sold. Prefill read it as profit. He has thirty days to notice, and silence counts as agreement.

As it is

Sub: Communication of proposed adjustment u/s 143(1)(a) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The return for AY 2026-27 filed vide ack. no. 4718220293280726 has been processed and the following variance(s) requiring adjustment u/s 143(1)(a)(ii) has/have been identified. Inconsistency between the income/deduction disclosed in the return and the information available in Form 26AS/AIS in respect of income chargeable under the head 'Capital Gains'. Variance: Rs. 1,10,000/-. You are required to respond within 30 days from the date of issue of this communication, failing which the adjustment shall be made and the return processed accordingly. DIN: ITBA/AST/S/143(1)(a)/2026-27/1078342219(1)

Nothing here says what he is accused of, what it costs, or what to do.

As it could be

The department thinks you left out ₹1,10,000 of share profit.

What this is based on

Meridian Securities Pvt Ltd reported it on 30 June 2026 · SEBI INZ000123456 · it appears in your statement as “Sale of securities”.

What they've got wrong: that ₹1,10,000 is the total value of everything you sold — not what you made on it. Across those trades you lost ₹4,200.

If you say nothing by 10 September, ₹1,10,000 is added to your income and about ₹34,300 comes out of your refund.

Reference number on this letter:
ITBA/AST/S/143(1)(a)/2026-27/1078342219(1)
Every letter from the department must carry one. Without it, the letter officially does not exist — CBDT Circular 19/2019. Almost no citizen knows they hold that right, so we print it and let them check it.

Same letter. The claim, its source, the error in it, and the price of silence.

His second letter is a s.245 set-off: ₹18,740 of this year's refund held against a demand from 2019-20 that he was never served. That screen carries a one-tap “I never received notice of this”, and says why the button exists — CAG Report 14/2024 found 7,341 duplicate demands overstating ₹15,652 crore.

then about 4 days

The account you chose can't receive the money.

Godavari Gramin Bank became part of Deccan Union Bank last year. Your account number is fine — the code that routes money to it is not.

then about 2 days

Returns filed the same week as yours are being processed now. Expect 10 to 14 days once both of those are cleared.

Every hold names the action that releases it. Clear both and the refund moves on.

Refund variance is what generates grievance volume, not duration — seven days for one return and three months for an identical one. So the promise is a stated range for your filing cohort, never a countdown. And a hold a citizen cannot act on is just opacity with more words, which is why every hold in the data model is required to carry a resolution path.

Act 3 · The wait, made legible

28 · Junior architect, first time filing · Pune, Maharashtra

Filed 71 days ago. The portal says “Under processing” and nothing else. Two separate things are actually holding her ₹28,400.

Refund variance drives grievance volume — seven days for one return, three months for an identical one. Meanwhile Revalidate does not work, and forums pass around delete-and-re-add as the fix.

DEMPS9052M

Every PAN begins DEMP. That is the deliberate tell — the fourth character is P, which is where a real PAN encodes an individual, so the format is right and the identity is unmistakably fictional. Mobile numbers use a repeating 90000 000NN pattern, because any realistic-looking Indian mobile number is somebody's and India has no reserved fiction range the way +1 has 555. Employers, banks, brokers and their TAN, IFSC and SEBI identifiers are invented too — so the disclaimer at the foot of every screen is literally true rather than approximately true.

Schedule I Infrastructure break it on purpose

An architecture argument you can press, instead of one you have to read.

A clearly-labelled demo control takes down each dependency in turn. Nothing errors. Each failure degrades into something the citizen can still act on — which is the whole claim, made checkable.

All dependencies healthy. Press one above to take it down.

UIDAI slow · session held

Your code is taking longer than usual to arrive. Nothing you've entered will be lost while you wait — this page stays open as long as it needs to. You can ask for another code in 15 seconds.

TRACES down · stale prefill, timestamped

These figures are as of 18 July. The system that holds your deduction records hasn't answered since this morning. You can carry on and confirm what's here, or wait and we'll tell you when it's fresh.

Gateway timeout · the third payment state

Payment received · challan pending. Your bank has taken the money. The receipt hasn't come back yet. Do not pay again — we're holding your reference and will attach the receipt when it arrives, usually within an hour.

That third one matters most. The real portal has no vocabulary for paid but unreceipted, so it shows failure and people pay twice. A payment saga with an idempotency key is not an implementation detail here — it is the difference between one debit and two.

Part C How this is scored six criteria, six answers

Each criterion gets one thing in the build that answers it, not a paragraph that claims it.

Judging criteria → the feature that answers it
CriterionWhat in the build answers it
Problem 66.5% of filers owed nothing last year. The product opens on “Is the department holding money that's yours?” — not on “File your return.” 5.58 cr of 8.39 cr
Working build Three logins, three complete journeys, live URL, no access request. Not a slide deck and not a click-through prototype — real routes, real state transitions, real language switching.
Usability Built at 375px first. Confirm-don't-compose, so the primary interaction is a two-button decision rather than a blank field. Full Tamil and Hindi interface, and speech input so nobody has to type.
Product thinking Provenance. Every figure names who reported it, under which identifier, and on what date. The real portal shows numbers with no origin — so a citizen who spots an error cannot tell whose mistake they are arguing with.
End-to-end thinking The break-it toggle in Schedule I. Payment saga with an idempotency key, per-dependency circuit breakers, cached prefill served with its own timestamp. An infrastructure argument you can press.
Honesty A route, not a footnote. /honesty lists what is real, what is synthetic, what is stubbed, and what making it real would cost — including that the Hindi and Tamil were written by us and not reviewed by a native speaker.

Honesty is the criterion most submissions will not attempt, because the incentive at a hackathon runs the other way. It is also the cheapest one to win outright — and a product about a system that overstates its own state should not overstate its own.

Part D Time today plus six, and the deadline has no grace period

Deploy on day one. The public link is the deliverable most likely to fail last.

Sat 22 Scaffold, design system, three languages, the three citizens. First Vercel deploy today — so the link exists from hour one.
Sun 23 Act 1 whole: provenance cards, “this is wrong”, confirm & file, verification as consequence.
Mon 24 Act 3: the refund state machine, named holds, the unblock, bank revalidation that works.
Tue 25 Act 2: notice inbox, decoding, guided disagree, the s.245 set-off screen.
Wed 26 Voice, Tamil and Hindi pass, break-it toggle, /honesty, /architecture, mobile polish.
Thu 27 Code freeze. Record the video. Write the 240 words. Walk all three personas on a clean device.
Fri 28 Submit in the morning. 20:00 IST, no grace period — and deadline evening is exactly when things break, which is what this whole project is about.

Act 3 is built before Act 2 on purpose. If one act has to slip, the journey still has to be complete — Acts 1 and 3 alone are a submittable end-to-end story, and Act 3 is also the strongest twenty seconds of the video.

Verification Declaration the risks, stated before they're discovered

I declare that what is written above is true to the best of my knowledge, and that the following four things are the parts most likely to go wrong. They are stated here so that neither of us can be surprised by them later.

  1. The brief wants Codex or an OpenAI model involved. We are building with Claude Code and hold no OpenAI key, so as planned the submission satisfies neither branch of that requirement. The mitigation is preserved by design rather than bolted on: notice-decoding and dispute-drafting sit behind an ExplanationProvider interface with a deterministic template implementation, so a key makes it genuinely model-powered in one file. Whether to take that is a decision, not an oversight.
  2. Three acts in six days is the real risk. The ordering above is the mitigation: Acts 1 and 3 alone are a complete, submittable journey. If Tuesday slips, Act 2 ships as a read-only decoded inbox without the guided disagree — and /honesty says exactly that, rather than the demo quietly avoiding the button.
  3. The Hindi and Tamil are ours, not a native speaker's. Interface strings are typed against the English dictionary, so a missing translation fails the build rather than falling back silently to English — but correctness of shape is not correctness of language. Disclosed on /honesty. A reviewer's proofread on Wednesday is worth more than anything else we could spend that hour on.
  4. “Wapsi” is a Hindi word in a product that also speaks Tamil. Common and defensible, and the double meaning is the whole pitch — वापसी is both the return you file and the money coming back. Say now if you'd rather a neutral mark: it touches the language files and the metadata, and nothing else.
